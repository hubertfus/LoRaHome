# v0.3.0-beta — reliability

> **Errata — use `v0.3.1-beta` instead.** This tag does not build on a clean
> Linux checkout: six of seven native harnesses fail on a missing POSIX feature
> macro, and three host tests hang on an `unref`'d timer. Both were found by the
> first CI run after tagging and are fixed in `v0.3.1-beta`, which is otherwise
> identical — same behaviour, same measurements, same Definition of Done status.
> The tag is left in place rather than moved, because it is what was released.

**Motto:** *Radio loses packets. Always. We design for a world where 10% of frames never arrive.*

Etap 2 is implemented, tested and measured. Six of the eight Definition of Done
items are closed. One is closed by arithmetic rather than by the number the
roadmap named, and is written up honestly below; one needs two boards in a field
for a week and cannot be closed from a desk.

The stage also found and fixed a mistake it inherited. Etap 1 closed by
correcting the airtime of a 230 B frame at SF9 from ~390 ms to **1147.9 ms**,
and warned that "anything sized against a 390 ms frame needs re-deriving". The
ARQ's 2000 ms retransmission timeout was one of those things. Against a round
trip that physically cannot complete in under 2295.8 ms, it fired while the
acknowledgement was still in the air — **0.6 wasted retransmissions per frame on
a clean link**, each one 1147.9 ms of airtime against a budget that permits one
frame every 115 s. Fixed by deriving the timeout from the airtime rather than
choosing it.

## The three defences, and what each one catches

Reliability here is not one mechanism. It is three, and each catches a failure
the others structurally cannot:

- **The frame CRC** catches corruption in flight. It cannot tell you that a
  perfectly transmitted frame is the *same* frame you already processed.
- **The duplicate window** catches that. It cannot tell you that two perfectly
  transmitted, perfectly distinct fragments belong to different configs.
- **`crc_total` over the assembled config** catches that. The harness forges
  exactly this case — fragments whose headers agree on every field and whose
  payloads do not — and requires it to be caught.

## Known issue, deferred deliberately

The full retry schedule spans 124 s (4+8+16+32+64), while an incomplete
reassembly is expired after 30 s. A config fragment needing more than two
retransmissions therefore outlives the receiver's window. Both numbers come
from the roadmap and both predate the airtime correction. Reconciling them
belongs with the config OTA path in Etap 4, which is the first thing that will
exercise it; guessing now would be choosing a number with no traffic to test it
against. Recorded in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §9.3.

## Two measurements reported under both models

Delivery figures are usually quoted with reliable acknowledgements. A real link
loses ACKs too, and a lost ACK is indistinguishable from a lost frame at the
sender. Both models are measured and neither is presented as the other:

| Scenario | Lossy ACKs | Reliable ACKs |
|---|---|---|
| Chaos, 30% loss | 99.939% | 99.933% |
| ARQ bench, 30% loss | 97.8% | 100.0% |
| ARQ bench, 50% loss | 81.6% | 98.6% |

Everything above was measured on `win32-x64`, Node 24.18.0, GCC 16.1.0
(MSYS2 UCRT64), PlatformIO 6.1.19, with the ESP cross toolchains at 8.4.0
(`esp-2021r2-patch5`). Sanitized runs use MSVC's ASAN. Chaos seed `0x8f2a`;
LoRa profile SF9/BW125/CR4-5, airtime per Semtech AN1200.22.

No target hardware was attached. Every on-target budget in this stage is
reported as `SKIPPED` with its budget intact rather than filled in with a host
number that would clear it by two orders of magnitude.
